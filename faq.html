Frequently asked questions

General

Does Kite work outside KDE Plasma?
Yes, on any Linux desktop. Plasma-specific features such as KRunner search, KDE Connect, KDE Wallet and the Breeze look need Plasma to be there.
Is there a Windows, macOS or mobile version?
No. Kite is Linux only.
Which extensions can I use?
Firefox add-ons from addons.mozilla.org, both Manifest V2 and V3. Kite covers the common WebExtensions APIs, but not all of Firefox's: add-ons that need containers, find, dns, themes or filterResponseData won't work fully. Chrome Web Store extensions aren't supported.
Are plugins safe to install?

Only install plugins from people you trust. Unlike Firefox add-ons, a native plugin runs inside Kite with no sandbox, signing or review. Once switched on, it can read your saved logins, cookies and history, and see and change every page, including in private windows. Installing one is like installing a program.

Kite asks before anything runs: an installed plugin stays off until you turn it on in Settings → Plugins & Extensions. If an add-on from addons.mozilla.org can do the job, prefer it, as add-ons only get the permissions they ask for.

Which plugins come with Kite?

Mouse gestures, Vim keys, Reader View, Picture-in-Picture, Plasma Integration and YouTube Download. Switch them on or off in Settings → Plugins & Extensions, or from the command palette. Each has its own settings page there.

YouTube Download adds a button to the address bar on YouTube videos, which saves the video in the quality you choose. It's off by default and needs yt-dlp installed, and ffmpeg for the best quality. Without yt-dlp the button doesn't show.

Why is my private window purple?
So you can tell it from a normal window at a glance. Kite colours a private window's toolbars, tab bar and, on Plasma, its titlebar a dark violet. To turn it off, untick "Purple chrome" in Settings → Privacy → Private Windows.

Can I bring my bookmarks and passwords from another browser?

Yes. The first time you start Kite, it looks for other browsers on your computer and offers to import their bookmarks: Firefox, LibreWolf, Waterfox, Chrome, Chromium, Brave, Edge, Vivaldi, Opera and Falkon, including Flatpak and Snap installs. You can come back to that page any time at kite:welcome.

Bookmarks can also be imported from an exported HTML file or Falkon's bookmarks.json. Passwords come in from CSV files (Firefox, Chrome, Bitwarden) or Falkon's XML export.

Kite doesn't show up in KRunner or the application launcher.
After installing, restart KRunner once with systemctl --user restart plasma-krunner. The application launcher picks Kite up at your next login.

Known issues

After tightening privacy settings, some sites stopped working.

The stricter you set Kite, the more sites break. Common examples:

  • Embedded YouTube videos don't play, or show an error.
  • Signing in to Google services (Gmail, YouTube, Drive) fails or loops.
  • Maps don't load, or show blank tiles.
  • Video calls can't connect.

The usual culprits are blocking all cookies, fingerprint protection, strict WebRTC settings and aggressive filter lists. Rather than loosening them everywhere, use the site settings button in the address bar to relax them for the site that's misbehaving, and try a private window to check whether your settings are the cause.

A site says my browser isn't supported, or refuses to load.

Some sites check the user agent, the name your browser gives itself, and turn away browsers they don't recognise. Kite can give a different one, for all sites in Settings → Browsing, or for one site with the site settings button in the address bar. Choose a common browser's, Kite's own (naming QtWebEngine), or type your own. Pages see the same name the server does.

Some sites need it the other way round: invent.kde.org, for one, turns away the common user agent and lets Kite's own through.

A site won't let me right-click or select text.
Kite doesn't let pages keep either from you. When a page cancels the right-click menu without showing one of its own, Kite opens its menu anyway, and Shift+right-click always opens Kite's. Text a page blocks from being selected can still be selected and copied. Both are on by default and can be turned off in Settings → Browsing. Web apps' own menus and controls are left alone.
Kite crashed. How do I report it?

The next time you start Kite, it shows a report of the crash, with buttons to view it, copy it, or open its folder. You can find it again under Help, in the command palette, or in Settings → General. Please open an issue and attach it.

Nothing is sent anywhere. The report and Kite's log stay in ~/.local/share/kite/logs/<profile>/, and addresses from private windows are scrambled in both.

Picture-in-picture doesn't work on some videos.
Kite brings its own picture-in-picture, since QtWebEngine has none. It doesn't work for videos inside frames, and sites can't start it themselves.

Known limitations

Why isn't WebGPU available?
Kite's engine is QtWebEngine, which is built from Chromium. Chromium doesn't enable WebGPU on Linux yet, so Kite can't either. It will arrive once Chromium ships it on Linux and QtWebEngine catches up.
Why is Kite's engine older than Chrome's?
QtWebEngine follows Chromium a few releases behind, and Kite gets newer engines as new Qt versions come out. Qt backports Chromium's security fixes in between.
Is there sync, passkeys or translation?
Not yet. Kite has no account system, so no sync between devices. Passkeys (WebAuthn), card autofill, built-in translation and installing sites as apps are also missing.
Can I use browser themes?
No. Kite follows your KDE colour scheme and icon theme instead. Change those in System Settings and Kite follows along.
